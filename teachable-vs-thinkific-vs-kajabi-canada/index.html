Best for an all-in-one approach (courses + email + funnels) if budget isn’t your main constraint.

SEO strategy that usually wins

If SEO is part of your acquisition plan, you typically want your main site to carry your authority (fast, optimized, full control), then use your course platform for checkout and student experience. In practice, this reduces platform lock-in and improves long-term scalability.

Scenario A: SEO-first creator

Build content + authority on your own domain. Use the platform as the purchase + student layer.

  • Best when you want long-term organic growth
  • Best when you care about performance and structure

Scenario B: Speed-first creator

Launch on a platform fast, validate the offer, then optimize later.

  • Best when you need revenue fast
  • Best when you don’t want to manage tech

FAQ (Canada)

Is Teachable worth it in Canada?

It can be, especially if your priority is launching quickly and focusing on marketing. If your strategy is heavily SEO-driven, consider using your main site for content and authority, and Teachable for checkout and course delivery.

Which platform is best for SEO?

In most cases, the best SEO setup is your own fast, optimized domain for content, with the course platform used as a conversion/delivery layer. If you want more platform-side flexibility, Thinkific often feels less restrictive than others.

Should I start on a platform or build custom?

If you need speed and clarity, start on a platform and validate the offer. If you already have traffic and a clear product, a hybrid approach (your site for SEO, platform for checkout/delivery) is usually the best long-term play.

Can I switch platforms later?

Usually yes, but migrations take time (content, students, offers, email sequences). That’s why a “site-first for SEO” strategy can reduce lock-in: you keep acquisition on your own domain and switch the delivery layer if needed.
