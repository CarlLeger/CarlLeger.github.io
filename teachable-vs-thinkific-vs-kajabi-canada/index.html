How to Pick (Without Overthinking)

A practical way to choose is to decide what you’re optimizing for right now: speed, control, or all-in-one marketing. You can evolve later, especially if you keep your content and audience on your own domain.

1) Speed to launch

If you want to ship quickly and focus on selling, pick the option that feels easiest to operate week-to-week.

2) Control and structure

If your product is curriculum-heavy, prioritize the platform that best fits your learning experience and organization needs.

3) Marketing stack

If you want email + funnels inside the same tool, it can reduce tool sprawl, but only if you’ll actually use it consistently.

Deep Dive (Practical, Canada-friendly)

Teachable

A strong pick when speed matters and you want a clean workflow from offer to checkout to course delivery.

  • Strengths: simple operations, fast setup, lower day-to-day overhead
  • Tradeoffs: customization and SEO are more “platform-shaped” than “developer-shaped”
  • Best for: validating an offer, creators shipping their first course

Thinkific

A good fit if you want more control over your learning experience and course structure without building custom.

  • Strengths: flexibility for curriculum, solid builder, good structure options
  • Tradeoffs: more decisions to make during setup
  • Best for: curriculum-heavy offers and creators who care about student UX

Kajabi

Best for an all-in-one workflow (courses + email + funnels) when you’re ready to run consistent marketing.

  • Best for: mature offers, teams, creators running campaigns regularly

FAQ (Canada)

Is Teachable worth it in Canada?

It can be, especially if your priority is launching quickly and focusing on marketing. If your strategy is SEO-heavy, use your main site for content + authority, and the platform for checkout/delivery.

Which platform is best for SEO?

Often the best approach is hybrid: publish content on your own fast domain, then use the platform for conversion and delivery. If you want more flexibility inside the platform itself, Thinkific often feels less restrictive for structure.

Should I start on a platform or build custom?

If you need speed, start on a platform and validate. If you already have traffic and a clear product, a hybrid (site for SEO, platform for delivery) often scales well long-term.

Can I switch platforms later?

Usually yes, but migrations take time (content, students, offers, email sequences). Keeping acquisition on your own domain reduces lock-in.
